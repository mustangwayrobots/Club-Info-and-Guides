# Soldering Safety SOP

How to solder safely when building club PCBs and wiring. Soldering irons run hot enough to cause serious burns in a split second, and solder and flux give off fumes you shouldn't breathe.

## 1. Before you can solder

1. Signed **Safety Acknowledgment Form** on file.
2. Soldering demonstration and practice with the advisor or a trained mentor.
3. An adult must be in the room whenever an iron is on.

## 2. The hazards

| Hazard | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Burns** | The iron tip is around 300–400 °C (600–750 °F). Melted solder and freshly soldered joints are also hot. |
| **Fumes** | The smoke is mostly from **flux**. It can irritate eyes, nose and lungs, and can trigger asthma. |
| **Lead** | Some solder contains lead. Lead doesn't come from the smoke at soldering temperature: it gets in your body from your **hands to your mouth**. |
| **Flying bits** | Clipped component leads and splashes of solder can hit your eyes. |
| **Fire** | A hot iron left on paper, foam, plastic or a cable can start a fire or melt the cable. |

## 3. Set up your station

* Work on a **heat-resistant mat** on a clear, uncluttered table.
* The iron must sit in its **stand** whenever it isn't in your hand.
* Turn on the **fume extractor** (fan with filter) and place it close to the work, pulling smoke away from your face.
* Keep the iron's cable where it can't be caught, tripped over, or touched by the hot tip.
* The club uses **lead-free solder** when possible. If leaded solder is used, it is labeled.
* Clamp the board in a **holder or "helping hands"**. Never hold the part with your fingers.

## 4. While soldering

* **Wear safety glasses.** Every time, including when clipping leads.
* Tie back long hair and loose sleeves. Remove dangling jewelry and lanyards.
* **Never** touch the metal part of the iron, or test if it's hot with your hand. Assume it is always hot.
* **Never** wave the iron around, flick solder off it, or hand a hot iron to someone. Put it in the stand and let them pick it up.
* Keep your face back from the smoke. Don't lean directly over the joint.
* Clip leads **pointing down** toward the table, and hold the end so it doesn't fly.
* **No food, drinks, gum or vapes** at the soldering station.
* Don't solder a board that has a battery or USB power connected. Unplug first.
* Never solder directly to a battery (see the Battery Safety Policy).

## 5. When you finish

1. **Turn off and unplug** the iron, and put it in its stand to cool.
2. Turn off the fume extractor.
3. Throw away solder scraps and clipped leads in the marked container, not on the floor.
4. Wipe the table.
5. **Wash your hands with soap and water** before eating, drinking or touching your face. This matters most with leaded solder.

## 6. If something goes wrong

* **Burn:** cool it right away under **cool running water for at least 10–20 minutes**. Don't use ice, butter or ointment. Tell an adult and go to the school nurse.
* **Something in your eye:** don't rub. Rinse with water or the eyewash station and get an adult.
* **Fire or melted cable:** unplug the iron if you can do so safely. Tell an adult. Follow the school's fire procedure for anything larger than a tiny smoldering spot.
* **Feeling dizzy, headache or trouble breathing:** stop, get fresh air and tell an adult.
* **Report** every burn or incident to the advisor the same day.

## 7. Advisor checklist

- [ ] Irons with stands, tip cleaners and heat mats in good condition (no damaged cables).
- [ ] Working fume extractors, filters replaced on schedule.
- [ ] Safety glasses for every station.
- [ ] Lead-free solder stocked; any leaded solder clearly labeled.
- [ ] Scrap container for solder and clipped leads.
- [ ] Hand-washing sink and soap available.
- [ ] Students trained before first use; training recorded.
